Job titles vary between employers. A junior developer at one company may maintain an existing service; another may expect broad ownership with little support. Compare the work and supervision rather than assuming a title describes the whole role.

## Five directions to explore

| Role | Typical work | A small project that demonstrates it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software developer | Implement, debug, test, and maintain application behavior | A working form with validation, failure handling, and tests |
| Data analyst | Clean data, define metrics, and explain findings | An analysis that documents missing data and checks its conclusions |
| Technical support | Reproduce user problems and communicate recovery steps | A troubleshooting guide tested by someone unfamiliar with the system |
| QA tester or analyst | Explore risks, reproduce defects, and verify changes | A test plan and clear bug reports for a demo application |
| UI/UX designer | Understand tasks, design interactions, and test usability | A prototype revised after observing users complete a task |

These are examples of responsibilities, not promises that every position is entry-level. The [BLS software and QA overview](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) is one source for occupational context in the United States; local expectations and training routes differ.

<Highlight color="lemon">Pick a role by the problems you want to practice, then check whether the employer provides the support expected at your level.</Highlight>

## Avoid false distinctions

Analysts write code, support engineers investigate systems, and developers test. QA is not simply clicking around, and UI/UX work is not only choosing colors. Collaboration matters because a feature can be technically correct while solving the wrong user problem.

For each direction, prepare evidence of reasoning: what was unclear, what you checked, what changed, and what remains limited. A small complete example is easier to discuss than a large unfinished project copied from a tutorial.

## Read the job description closely

Look for concrete responsibilities, review practices, onboarding, and the expected scope of independent decisions. Ask who will review your work, how questions are handled, and what a successful first few months would involve. An “entry-level” label paired with sole responsibility for a production system deserves clarification.

<Underline>Evidence and learning support matter more than collecting tool names.</Underline> Learn the tools needed for your chosen example, but explain the underlying decision when presenting it. Do not use confidential work or real customer data in a public portfolio.

## Try one task from two roles

Build a small feature, then test it as a QA exercise or observe someone using it as a design exercise. Notice which work holds your attention and where you need more practice. This is a low-cost way to compare directions before investing in a long course or collecting certifications without a target.

<RainbowHighlight block>Choose a starting role through its daily work, demonstrable skills, and available mentorship—not its title alone.</RainbowHighlight>
